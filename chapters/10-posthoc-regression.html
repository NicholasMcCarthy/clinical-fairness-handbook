10  Post-hoc methods: regression and uncertainty intervals

You should be able to distinguish equal prediction distributions from equal errors, interpret a transport map, and explain what group conformal coverage does and does not guarantee.

10.1 Three different goals

A regression audit may ask whether a model systematically underpredicts in one group, whether absolute errors differ, or whether the prediction distributions differ. These are different properties.

It may also ask whether prediction intervals contain future outcomes often enough. That concerns uncertainty coverage. An interval can improve coverage while leaving the point prediction completely unchanged.

10.2 Quantile transport

Let \(F_a\) be the distribution function of predicted values in group \(a\), and \(F_a^{-1}\) its quantile function. A person’s percentile within their group is \(F_a(s)\). A transport map replaces that score with the same percentile in a reference distribution.

For squared Wasserstein distance in one dimension,

\[ W_2^2(F_a,F_b)=\int_0^1\{F_a^{-1}(u)-F_b^{-1}(u)\}^2\,du. \]

Given nonnegative reference masses \(\pi_b\) summing to one, the Wasserstein barycenter has quantile function

\[ Q_B(u)=\sum_b\pi_b F_b^{-1}(u). \]

The full repair is

\[ g(s,a)=Q_B\{F_a(s)\}. \tag{10.1}\]

Under the continuous-distribution construction, this maps each group’s predictions to the same distribution. The squared-loss interpretation and optimality results in fair regression depend on the source paper’s assumptions (Chzhen et al. 2020).

In words. If a prediction is at the 70th percentile of its group’s predictions, move it to the 70th percentile of the chosen common distribution. The reference masses are part of the policy; equal group weighting and population weighting need not produce the same map.

The map can be fitted from scores and group membership without outcomes. Outcomes are still needed to evaluate errors or select a useful strength of repair. Applying the map needs group membership.

10.3 Partial repair

Full distributional parity may make an unacceptable change. Under the squared-Wasserstein convention used in the CFT paper, partial repair is

\[ g_\alpha(s,a)=\sqrt\alpha\,s+(1-\sqrt\alpha)g(s,a), \qquad 0\le\alpha\le1. \tag{10.2}\]

Here \(\alpha=0\) gives full repair and \(\alpha=1\) leaves the score unchanged. At \(\alpha=0.25\), the two predictions receive equal weight. Other implementations may parameterise interpolation differently, so always state the formula (Chzhen and Schreuder 2022).

The parameter controls this particular distributional change. It does not request a specified MAE difference or clinical benefit.

CFT scope. The native implementation estimates distributions on calibration predictions, with declared weights where supported. Deterministic mid-ranks handle ties, and endpoint quantiles handle scores outside calibration support. These choices matter for zero-heavy spending and tails. They do not inherit every continuous or jitter-based guarantee in the source literature (McCarthy 2026).

10.4 Clinical interpretation of distributional repair

Imagine a service using predicted expenditure to plan a budget. Equal prediction distributions might conflict with genuine differences in expected spending. If the same score is used as a proxy for unmet need, equalising expenditure predictions still may not correct the proxy problem.

Review signed error, MAE or RMSE, upper-tail errors, ranks, and downstream allocation. A transport map can be mathematically successful and clinically unhelpful. The long paper illustrates this with different patterns across diabetes progression and MEPS expenditure; it does not identify a universally preferred repair (McCarthy 2026).

10.5 Group split-conformal intervals

Conformal methods use a separate calibration sample to turn prediction errors into uncertainty sets. For a frozen point predictor \(f\) and group \(a\), define absolute calibration residuals

\[ r_i=|Y_i-f(X_i)|,\qquad i\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{cal},a}. \]

With \(n_a\) calibration observations and target miscoverage \(\eta\), use the order statistic

\[ k_a=\left\lceil(n_a+1)(1-\eta)\right\rceil,\qquad I_a(x)=[f(x)-r_{a,(k_a)},\ f(x)+r_{a,(k_a)}]. \tag{10.3}\]

If \(k_a>n_a\), take the radius as infinite. Replacing it with the largest finite residual would silently weaken the stated finite-sample construction.

Under exchangeability within the declared group, with the fitting/calibration separation required by the method, this construction gives

\[ P\{Y_{\mathrm{new}}\in I_a(X_{\mathrm{new}})\mid A_{\mathrm{new}}=a\}\ge1-\eta. \]

This is a group-conditional marginal statement over the calibration sample and new observation. It is not a guarantee for each individual, each covariate value, or every realised calibration sample. It also does not promise equal interval widths (Romano et al. 2020).

10.6 A small conformal example

Suppose there are 19 calibration residuals in a group and the target coverage is 90%. Then \(k=\lceil20\times0.9\rceil=18\). If the 18th residual is 12 units, the interval around a prediction of 50 is \([38,62]\).

With only four residuals, \(k=\lceil5\times0.9\rceil=5\). There is no fifth observed residual, so the stated construction requires an unbounded interval. These invented values illustrate why nominal coverage and practical usefulness must be reported together.

An interval that is wide enough to include almost every plausible outcome may be statistically conservative but unhelpful for a clinical decision. Compare coverage, width, and how those vary across groups and outcome ranges.

10.7 What changes in real datasets?

The CFT implementation uses symmetric absolute-residual intervals. Romano and colleagues’ equalized-coverage work includes more flexible quantile-based constructions. The simple adapter should not be described as a reproduction of every method in that paper.

Repeated observations require a unit-specific contract. Using a maximum residual per cluster/group changes the target to simultaneous coverage for that group’s members of a new exchangeable cluster. It is not the same as individual-row coverage.

Arbitrary survey weights do not by themselves establish weighted conformal validity. A sampling or likelihood-ratio argument is required. The current CFT adapter rejects unsupported nonuniform weighting rather than quietly discarding it. Distribution shift can also break exchangeability; a 90% label is not a permanent property of the interval (McCarthy 2026).
